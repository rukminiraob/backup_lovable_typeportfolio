01 Context

Missed collections.
Zero visibility.

Residents faced significant frustration with daily garbage collection. The lack of accurate arrival times resulted in either long, inconvenient wait times or missed pickups entirely, decreasing the overall waste disposal efficiency in the city.

02The Problem

The Tracking Gap.

The Core Issue

The trucks run on a fixed municipal schedule. Residents have no access to it. The result: people wait outside 20–40 minutes, or give up and leave garbage on the street. This isn't a behaviour problem — it's an information gap with a design solution.
03Process & Learnings

What I learned in the process.

Mapping the "hearing-distance" of a truck vs. resident response time revealed that the 15-minute awareness gap was the primary cause of urban dumping.

Insight 01
🚦

The Awareness Barrier

It wasn't about apathy—it was about uncertainty. Digital alerts bridged the 15-minute gap where residents previously relied on hearing the truck from indoors.

Insight 02
👥

Multi-Stakeholder Needs

Designing for diverse personas—from elderly residents with limited mobility to busy working professionals—required a UI that prioritized high-contrast visibility and immediate notifications.

Insight 03
🗺️

Critical Path Logistics

To deliver in one week, I focused strictly on the two-sided coordination between the Driver App and Resident App, mapping the end-to-end logistics that build civic trust.

04Design Logic

The Smart Waste Architecture.

There are two necessary streams: the user-facing resident app, and the underlying municipal architecture connecting the trucks.

1

Live GPS

Garbage trucks are equipped with GPS tracking broadcasting securely in real-time.

Eliminates: Complete uncertainty
2

Proximity Alerts

The system actively monitors proximity and sends automated push alerts as the truck approaches the user's specific area.

Eliminates: Time-consuming waiting
3

Short-Range ETAs

ETAs shown only within a 20-minute window. Long-range predictions were scrapped — route mapping showed traffic variability made them unreliable, and a wrong ETA erodes trust faster than no ETA at all.

Eliminates: Guesswork without creating false certainty
4

Interactive Map

A clear map interface for easy navigation and visual confirmation by residents.

Eliminates: Confusion

06Reflections & Artifacts

Behind the decisions.

Failed Idea
❌

Precise 2-hour ETAs

Initial research suggested residents wanted minute-by-minute countdowns. However, route mapping revealed that traffic variability made long-range predictions unreliable, leading to a loss of user trust.

Tradeoff Decision
⚖️

Geo-fencing vs. Live Polling

Chose geo-fenced proximity alerts over constant live-map polling. This reduced municipal server load and accounted for local infrastructure gaps while still providing the essential "take action" signal.

Future Iteration
🚀

Citizen-Sourced Validation

Implement a loop where residents confirm truck arrivals, creating a self-correcting feedback loop that improves scheduling accuracy without requiring expensive hardware upgrades.

01The Brief

8 hours.
Trustworthy long-term help.

This 8-hour design sprint was dedicated to solving the trust gap in informal hiring within apartment communities. Currently, hiring happens via unreliable word-of-mouth networks without solid ways to verify background or reliability.

I walked down to my apartment lobby and interviewed 5 residents. The finding was unanimous: nobody wanted an algorithm to hire a maid, they only trusted real human vouches.

The Problem Statement

"Residents are hesitant to hire long-term help because they are forced to rely on informal channels with zero ways to actually verify backgrounds."
M

Homemaker. Hired 4 maids in 2 years. Each left without notice.

02The Tension

Two sides. One shared goal.

Apartment Resident
  • Requires trustworthy, verified household help
  • Desires long-term commitment (6 months – 1 year)
vs.
Household Worker
  • Needs consistent contract-based work
  • Seeks robust job security
03Discovery

The Feasibility Pivot.

💡

The core breakthrough to maintain feasibility was focusing on leveraging local apartment community networks. By sourcing background verifications and reviews directly from neighbors, we bypass expensive third-party checks while establishing authentic, localized trust.

→

04Design Logic

Key App Features.

The wireframes prioritize trust-building mechanisms above all else, establishing confidence before contracting even begins through a strict profile-based selection process.

📄

Contract-Based Hiring

Clear structures supporting 6-month to 1-year agreements, stabilizing expectations for everyone.

🛡️

Verified Background Checks

Integrated verification processes leveraging verified apartment network credentials securely.

⭐

User-Driven Rating System

Reliable performance reviews and recommendations crowdsourced exclusively from neighbors.

⚠️

Anti-Exploitation Loops

Workers rate residents too. Households with a history of wage delays or abuse are mathematically locked out of the hiring pool. The system protects both sides.

06Reflections & Artifacts

Behind the decisions.

Failed Idea
❌

Full API Background Checks

I initially aimed for full tech ID verification. I had to scrap it because it added massive friction and alienated the target informal laborers.

Tradeoff Decision
⚖️

6-Month Minimum Friction

Enforced strict 6-month minimum hiring contracts. It significantly slowed down initial hiring but drastically improved lifetime reliability.

Future Iteration
🚀

Preventing Fake Profiles

Relying on physical guard ledgers works for scale 0 to 1, but guards can be bribed. A secondary cryptographic validation loop would be needed to prevent identity spoofing as the system scales.
