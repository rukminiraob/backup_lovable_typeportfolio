The Challenge

Every morning, residents wait without knowing when garbage will be collected. The real problem isn't waste collection—it's the lack of visibility and communication.

01. Unpredictable Collection: Never know when the truck will arrive.
02. Missed Pickups: Busy schedules lead to missed disposal.
03. Poor Communication: No timely updates or ways to report issues.

Research & Insights

15 Residents
1 Wk Research
4 Competitors
3 Insights
01. Residents prioritize reliable 10-minute alerts over live tracking.
02. Missed pickups happen due to mismatched dual-income schedules.
03. Trust is broken; reporting feels like shouting into a void.

"Users weren't asking for another app. They were asking for predictability."

View Detailed Design Strategy ↓
User Insight
Design Decision
Expected Impact

Residents miss garbage collection because truck arrival times are unpredictable.

Introduce live truck tracking with estimated arrival times.

Help residents plan ahead and reduce missed pickups.

Residents receive no communication about delays or completed collections.

Provide proactive notifications and collection status updates.

Increase transparency and build trust in the service.

Reporting missed collections is confusing and time-consuming.

Design a simple one-tap issue reporting flow.

Reduce reporting friction and improve issue resolution.

Impact & Reflection

Outcome: Improved visibility into waste schedules, reducing resident uncertainty and reporting friction.

View Outcomes & Learnings ↓

Project Outcome

  • Research: Interviewed 15 residents across 3 neighborhoods to map disposal habits.
  • Design: Designed 11 high-fidelity mobile app screens covering tracking and reporting flows.
  • Validation: Validated through usability testing, reducing task complexity for issue reporting.
  • Learning: Reduced uncertainty by prioritizing reliable 10-minute alerts over pure live tracking.

Key Learnings

  • Civic problems are often communication problems rather than technology problems.
  • Designing for trust creates more value than adding more features.
  • Small moments of certainty can significantly improve everyday experiences.

Sahayak

Marketplace UX Trust Design Consumer Product Booking Experience

"Reliable Help for Your Modern Home" — a trust-first domestic services platform that connects urban families with verified helpers. Designed in an intense 1-day design sprint with full UX documentation.

↑ 8 screens shown above — Onboarding & Discovery · Home Dashboard · Profile Details · Trial Booking · Trial Confirmation · Contract Management · Ongoing Management · Exit Page

Open Live App ↗
Problem No reliable way to find or manage domestic help in Indian urban homes.
Solution A trust-first platform built around verification, trial bookings, and transparent contracts.
My Role UX research, persona creation, journey mapping, wireframes, and high-fidelity UI design.
Tools Figma · FigJam · Stitch · GitHub
View Research & Insights ↓
10 Users Surveyed
1 Day Sprint
2 Key Insights
01. Families distrust domestic help due to lack of background verification and no accountability system.
02. A trial booking period was the single most-requested feature — it reduces the commitment risk before a long-term contract.

"Trust isn't built by a profile photo. It's built by a system that holds someone accountable."

View Design Decisions ↓
User Insight
Design Decision
Expected Impact

Families can't verify a helper's background before inviting them home.

Display verified badges, ratings, and work history prominently on every profile.

Reduce hesitation and build confidence before the first booking.

Users fear committing to a long-term contract without knowing if the helper is a good fit.

Introduce a "Trial Booking" flow — a low-risk, short-term engagement before a full contract.

Lower the barrier to first use and increase successful long-term placements.

No transparent mechanism to handle disputes or exit a bad arrangement.

Design a Contract Management and Exit Flow with clear steps and confirmation screens.

Empower users with control, making the platform feel safe and accountable.

Impact & Reflection

Outcome: Designed 8 end-to-end screens covering a full service journey in a 1-day sprint.

View Outcomes & Learnings ↓

Project Outcome

  • Research: Surveyed 10 urban households to uncover root causes of distrust in domestic services.
  • Design: Designed 8 end-to-end screens (discovery to exit) during an intense 1-day sprint.
  • Validation: Validated through usability testing that the "Trial Booking" flow increased confidence during booking.
  • Learning: Trust is established through system accountability (contracts/disputes) rather than just visual polish.

Key Learnings

  • Trust in a service platform is established through system accountability, not visual polish alone.
  • A low-commitment entry point (trial booking) removes the biggest barrier to first use in high-trust domains.
  • Compressing a full UX process into 1 day forces ruthless prioritisation — a valuable constraint.

Photon Avishkar · Team 5

HiBro

Outcome: Creative Finisher Time Taken: 2-Month Hackathon
The Problem

Men struggle to express emotions due to social conditioning, leading to silent suffering.

The Solution

Private tools to release, reflect, and connect anonymously without fear of judgment.

"He says I'm fine. But inside, he's drowning."

HiBro is a private emotional wellness app designed for men. Built during DFC Hackathon 8.0, it addresses the silent emotional crisis men face by providing anonymous tools to express, release, and heal without fear of judgment.

✦ Rage Scribble — Draw it out. No rules. Just release.
✦ Emotional Dump — Speak freely. No one listens. But it helps.
✦ Anonymous Community — Read, share, connect. You are not alone.
✦ Future Self AI — Chat with a wiser version of you. Get guidance. Gain clarity.
Problem Men struggle to express emotions due to social conditioning, leading to silent suffering.
Solution Private tools to release, reflect, and connect anonymously without fear of judgment.
My Role End-to-end product design, user research, wireframing, and interactive prototyping.
Tools Figma · FigJam · Rapid prototyping · Design Thinking
View Research & Insights ↓
2 Mths Hackathon
5 Team Members
3 Core Features
01. Men are conditioned to suppress emotions — existing wellness apps feel clinical, feminine-coded, or socially exposed.
02. Anonymous expression is not avoidance; it is a valid first step toward emotional processing for many men.
03. Trust in a platform is built through perceived privacy control, not just a privacy policy.

"He says I'm fine. But inside, he's drowning."

View Design Decisions ↓
User Insight
Design Decision
Expected Impact

Men fear being seen or judged when expressing vulnerability.

Build the core experience around anonymous, identity-free expression — no profile required to use core tools.

Remove the social barrier to entry so users engage before they commit.

Text-based journaling feels too structured and emotionally distant for many men.

Design "Rage Scribble" — a freehand drawing canvas as a non-verbal, low-friction emotional release.

Reach users who would never write a journal entry but will draw when frustrated.

Men feel isolated but are reluctant to seek live human support.

Introduce "Future Self AI" — a non-judgmental, reflective AI persona to guide introspection.

Provide a bridge between total isolation and formal therapy without stigma.

Impact & Reflection

Outcome: Conceptualised a full product solution and won the Creative Finisher award at DFC Hackathon 8.0.

View Outcomes & Learnings ↓

Project Outcome

  • Research: Analyzed mental health stigma in male populations to identify emotional disclosure barriers.
  • Design: Designed 3 core anonymous features (including Rage Scribble) over a 2-month hackathon sprint.
  • Validation: Won the Creative Finisher award at DFC Hackathon 8.0 out of multiple competing teams.
  • Learning: Removing visible identity entirely reduces the friction of first-time emotional expression.

Key Learnings

  • Designing for stigma-sensitive problems requires removing visible identity before anything else.
  • Non-verbal expression (drawing, voice) reaches users that text-first UX completely misses.
  • Hackathon constraints force you to define the single most important user problem and solve only that.

Product Thinking

Independent product observations identifying real-world experience gaps and proposing user-centered improvements.

The Cult.fit
experience gap.

Observation

Users successfully book a slot via the app, but arrive at the gym to find it overcrowded with no available machines or floor space.

User Problem

Gym-goers waste time waiting for equipment, disrupting their workout flow and creating a frustrating, unpredictable offline experience.

View Detailed Product Breakdown ↓
Why Existing Experience Fails

The app optimizes for digital booking limits but ignores the physical capacity constraints and real-time occupancy of the facility.

Opportunity

Bridge the gap between digital booking and physical reality by surfacing real-time gym conditions to users before they leave home.

Product Thinking

Leverage existing user check-in data to calculate live crowd density. Shift the app's role from a simple booking utility to a proactive habit-enabler.

Potential MVP

Display a simple "Live Crowd Level" indicator (Low/Medium/High) on the booking screen based on current check-ins minus average exit rates.

Expected UX Impact

Users can make informed decisions about when to visit, leading to a better offline experience, higher satisfaction, and stronger habit retention.

Validation Plan

A/B test the MVP to measure if crowd-awareness data actually shifts booking distributions from peak to off-peak hours.
